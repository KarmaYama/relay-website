A fast follow-up to beta 6, from a day of real testing. Three things were losing people's work, and one thing everybody eventually needs was missing.

Forgotten your password? There is now a way back in. Ask for a code, it arrives at the email address on your account, and you choose a new password. The code lasts fifteen minutes and gives you five tries.

Change your password from inside the app. Under Account. It asks for your current one first — a phone left unlocked on a table should not be enough for somebody to lock you out of Relay.

Either way, every device signed in to your account is signed out. A new password is worth nothing if whoever you are worried about simply stays signed in on the phone they already have.

Photos were being accepted and then lost. A picture would upload, look fine, and be gone a few hours later. It had been written somewhere temporary the whole time. This is fixed, but pictures taken before today are not recoverable, and we would rather say so than let you find out.

Your job disappeared the moment a runner started it. It was on the home screen saying "On the way" and in neither My Jobs nor History, so there was nowhere to open it. Every job now appears somewhere, always.

"Too many requests" while doing nothing unusual. Relay was asking itself the same question twice at once. It now asks far less often.

Steps of a job stopped being recorded halfway through. Delivery ticks did not stick, read receipts did not stick, a photo never attached itself to the message it answered, and a step could be raised but never marked answered or declined. None of it looked like a failure, which is why it lasted.

Show my ID no longer needs anyone else to be there. Runners are asked who they are by gate guards, receptionists and shopkeepers far more often than by the customer — and none of them have Relay. Your ID is now always available from your own menu, with no job and no signal needed. The matching code still appears underneath when there is someone to check it with.

Jobs with nobody behind them are cleared away. When somebody leaves Relay, the jobs they posted used to stay in the list. A runner could take one, travel to it, and find no customer and nobody to pay them. Work that has already started is deliberately left alone.

Accounts nobody has used for a month can be closed. An abandoned account still holds a name, a phone number and sometimes an ID document, with nobody watching it. Where somebody has been part of a job, the account closes but the record stays — a disagreement months later has to be decided on something.

Guessing somebody's password gets slower and slower. Five tries at full speed, which covers any normal mistype, and after that a wait that grows to fifteen minutes. Getting it right clears it instantly, and so does leaving it an hour. Relay will never lock you out of your own account for this — a lock would let anyone who knows your username shut you out on purpose, so the worst anybody can do is make you wait.

Keywords: password reset, sign-out everywhere, photo storage, job visibility, request limits, delivery ticks, ID badge, dormant accounts, sign-in attempts

Relay never holds your money

You pay your runner directly. There is no wallet, no escrow and no held balance. What Relay gives you is a verified identity on the other side of the job, a permanent timestamped record of what happened, and a dispute process staffed by people.

Seeing “Can't reach Relay”?

Turn off any VPN, particularly one that blocks ads. Those filter DNS for your whole phone, while your browser quietly routes around them — so this site loads normally and the app still cannot connect. Also set Private DNS to Automatic, or try switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data.
